# ADR Campus v0.6 — Supersede a Decision

Version 0.6 allows an active organization member to propose a new ADR as a replacement for a currently accepted decision, while preserving both records and the direct history between them. It delivers the journey defined by [Supersede a Decision](../stories/05-supersede-a-decision.md).

**Status:** Complete

The story document remains authoritative. This release record defines its delivery boundary and links the implementation backlog without restating every acceptance criterion.

## Release Outcomes

At the end of v0.6:

- an active member can begin a private replacement draft from an accepted ADR without changing the accepted decision;
- the draft author can select, change, or remove one valid intended supersession target while the replacement remains private;
- proposal freezes and shares the intended target only when that target is still accepted;
- accepting a replacement atomically accepts the new ADR, supersedes exactly one earlier ADR, and records reciprocal relationships with one supersession time;
- rejecting a replacement preserves its intended-target history and leaves the target accepted and unchanged;
- stale, cross-organization, self-referential, cyclic, and otherwise invalid relationships are refused without partial state;
- concurrent replacements resolve deterministically: the first accepted replacement wins and competing proposals remain proposed but cannot be accepted against the superseded target;
- supersession acceptance is idempotent and safe to retry after an uncertain outcome;
- direct supersession chains remain intact and navigable in both directions; and
- Current and Historical discovery immediately reflect which decision is in effect without erasing earlier content, attribution, or lifecycle history.

## Included Story

### Supersede a Decision

A replacement begins as a new private ADR with its own stable identity and an optional intended target. The target is editable only while the replacement remains a draft and must be a currently accepted ADR in the same organization whenever it is selected or proposed. Proposal makes the intended relationship shared and immutable but leaves the target accepted and in effect.

Acceptance revalidates the target and completes supersession as one operation. The replacement becomes accepted, the target becomes superseded, both records gain reciprocal direct relationships, and their lifecycle history records the same supersession time. Rejection records only the replacement's ordinary decision outcome and retains its intended-target context.

## State and Consistency Boundary

- Intended supersession is private Workbench metadata while the replacement is a draft.
- Intended supersession becomes immutable shared Library metadata when the replacement is proposed.
- Completed supersession is authoritative only after the replacement, target, reciprocal relationships, and lifecycle evidence commit together.
- The target is revalidated when selected, when the replacement is proposed, and when acceptance is committed.
- A failed command cannot expose a partial transition, a one-sided relationship, or contradictory discovery results.
- Client-generated operation identifiers make genuine retries return the original completed outcome without duplicating decisions, relationships, or history.
- Keycloak remains authoritative for current Member and Maintainer authority at command time.

## Deliberately Deferred

Version 0.6 does not include:

- replacing several ADRs with one proposal or replacing one ADR with several accepted ADRs as one operation;
- editing or retargeting a proposed replacement;
- automatically rejecting, merging, or retargeting competing proposals;
- directly superseding a proposed, rejected, or already-superseded ADR;
- undoing, reversing, or flattening a completed supersession chain;
- cross-organization supersession relationships;
- notifications, reviewer assignment, voting, or discussion; or
- draft recovery and remaining organization-administration behavior.

## Definition of Done

Version 0.6 is complete when:

1. every applicable acceptance criterion from story 05 passes automated or documented manual QA;
2. replacement drafts support adding, changing, and removing one valid intended target without changing any target record;
3. proposal freezes the intended target and refuses a target that is no longer accepted;
4. acceptance atomically transitions both ADRs, records reciprocal relationships and history, and uses one decision/supersession time;
5. rejection leaves the target accepted while preserving the replacement's intended-target context;
6. stale-target, cross-organization, self-reference, cycle, retry, concurrent-replacement, and partial-persistence scenarios pass automated tests;
7. direct supersession chains remain navigable without rewriting earlier links;
8. Current, Historical, All, search, detail, and lifecycle-history views consistently reflect completed supersession without exposing private drafts;
9. non-members learn no record or relationship metadata and every protected mutation rechecks current authority; and
10. the full solution test suite, solution build, restart verification, and `git diff --check` pass.

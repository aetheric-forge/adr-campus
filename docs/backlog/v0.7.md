# v0.7 Backlog — Complete Organization Administration

This backlog sequences the work required by the [v0.7 release](../releases/v0.7.md). [Administer the Organization](../stories/06-administer-the-organization.md) and the recovery rules in [Draft a Decision](../stories/01-draft-a-decision.md) remain authoritative.

## Milestone A — Durable Organization Foundation

### A1. Model organization administration state

- Add a stable organization projection containing its identity, current display name, immutable SSO authority and group mappings, version, and timestamps.
- Model application-administration events with stable actor or source references and effective identity values where applicable.
- Distinguish SSO-observed events from maintainer-initiated application events without inventing a human actor for external changes.
- Define provider contracts that can atomically persist an organization transition and its administration event.

### A2. Validate bootstrap

- Resolve the configured member and maintainer groups against the configured SSO authority.
- Require at least one enabled identity to belong to both groups and reject orphaned Maintainer membership.
- Create exactly one organization projection and bootstrap event for valid configuration.
- Make initialization idempotent across retries and restarts and refuse incompatible configuration without exposing a partially authorized organization.
- Preserve the rule that the first visitor receives no bootstrap authority.

### A3. Implement organization rename

- Normalize and validate a display name between 3 and 100 characters with at least one letter or number and no control characters.
- Allow only a currently authorized active Maintainer to rename the organization.
- Preserve the stable organization identity, SSO mappings, membership projections, and ADR ownership.
- Persist the previous name, new name, responsible Maintainer, change time, and operation identifier atomically.
- Protect rename against stale versions, retries, reused operation identifiers, and persistence failure.

### A4. Present settings safely

- Replace the organization-settings placeholder with a Maintainer-only current-state and rename experience.
- Make externally managed SSO mappings visibly read-only and avoid exposing privileged credentials or raw directory data.
- Explain invalid configuration and unavailable current authority without offering an application-side bypass.
- Use the persisted organization display name consistently in the application shell and administration pages.

### QA Checkpoint A — Organization State

- Valid bootstrap produces one durable organization and one bootstrap event across retries and restart.
- Missing, ambiguous, cross-authority, orphaned, or final-Maintainer-free configuration creates no usable partial projection.
- Rename normalization, validation, authorization, optimistic concurrency, retry, and injected-failure tests preserve the last coherent state.
- No application surface offers SSO identity or group mutation.

## Milestone B — Observe Membership and Preserve History

### B1. Model effective membership

- Persist one projection per stable external identity with effective non-member, Member, or Maintainer state and observation metadata.
- Retain stable identity references and effective-dated identifying properties needed for durable attribution without creating local user accounts.
- Define deterministic comparison of a current directory snapshot with the last coherent projection.
- Represent final-Maintainer loss and invalid group configuration explicitly rather than silently broadening authority.

### B2. Process directory observations

- Observe enabled member and maintainer group membership through the existing server-side directory integration.
- Atomically persist effective additions, removals, role changes, identity-property changes, and their administration events.
- Make repeated or overlapping observations idempotent and safe under concurrency.
- Preserve the last coherent projection when retrieval or persistence fails and fail closed for affected protected mutations.
- Record the SSO authority and observation time without claiming to know the external change time or operator.

### B3. Coordinate authority and presentation

- Keep protected mutations authorized against sufficiently current SSO-derived state rather than a stale page, session, or unbounded projection.
- Preserve ordinary Member access when only Maintainer authority is removed.
- Deny all organization access when membership is removed or the identity is disabled.
- Detect and prominently report the absence of an active Maintainer without automatically promoting a Member.
- Continue resolving current display names retroactively while preserving earlier identifying-property values on historical events.

### B4. Complete roster behavior

- Retain the existing Member-visible roster while sourcing role and identity presentation from the coherent current observation.
- Show each enabled Member exactly once and label Maintainers only when they are also Members.
- Exclude former or disabled identities without erasing their historical ADR attribution.
- Expose no recovery counts, administration history, raw claims, group identifiers, or privileged SSO data.
- Distinguish unavailable current directory data from a genuinely empty roster.

### QA Checkpoint B — Membership Observation

- Add, remove, disable, return, promote, demote, display-name change, and identifying-property change scenarios produce the expected effective state and one history event each.
- An identity in only the Maintainer group receives no access.
- Replayed snapshots, concurrent observations, directory failures, and persistence failures create no duplicate or partial transitions.
- Stale sessions cannot complete protected mutations after authority changes.
- Ordinary Members retain allowed access when no active Maintainer remains, but every Maintainer mutation fails closed.

## Milestone C — Draft Recovery and Reassignment

### C1. Start and cancel recovery windows

- Extend private-draft state with an optional recovery deadline and the departed author's stable identity.
- When membership loss is observed, start one 30-day window for every private draft still owned by that identity using the ADR Campus observation time.
- Commit the membership transition, affected draft changes, and administration history coherently.
- If the same identity returns before reassignment or expiry, cancel each open window and restore ordinary author access without duplicating identity or attribution.
- Do not reopen a reassigned or expired draft when a former member returns.

### C2. Build the recovery read model

- List only unexpired drafts whose authors are no longer active for a currently authorized Maintainer.
- Expose only identifier, title, former author, and expiration time until reassignment is confirmed.
- Keep recovery records out of shared discovery, search, counts, suggestions, and ordinary direct-record access.
- Resolve historical author display safely when the identity is no longer in the current roster.
- Degrade safely when current authority or identity presentation is unavailable.

### C3. Reassign a recoverable draft

- Require a currently authorized active Maintainer and a currently active Member as the new author.
- Recheck the draft's recovery state, deadline, version, former author, and recipient at commit time.
- Atomically preserve draft identity, content, creation time, status, and intended supersession target while changing authorship and clearing recovery state.
- Record former author, new author, responsible Maintainer, reassignment time, and operation identifier in draft and administration history.
- Make reassignment idempotent and concurrency-safe and prevent a returning former author or second Maintainer from reversing the winner.

### C4. Implement the recovery experience

- Replace the recovery placeholder with eligible-draft listing, recipient selection from the current safe roster, confirmation, and outcome guidance.
- Reveal no full draft content to the Maintainer unless they become the new author through a completed reassignment.
- Remove a successfully reassigned draft from recovery immediately and make it available only to its new author.
- Explain stale deadlines, returning authors, changed recipients, lost authority, concurrency, and retryable failure without leaking content.

### QA Checkpoint C — Recover Drafts

- Membership loss starts exactly one correctly timed window per eligible draft and leaves shared records unchanged.
- Return within the window restores the original author's access only when reassignment has not completed.
- Only an active Maintainer can list recovery metadata or reassign, and only to a current active Member.
- Reassignment preserves all draft content and identity, records both histories, and transfers ordinary author access exactly once.
- Concurrent return, reassignment, repeated operation, stale version, authority loss, and injected failure expose no partial ownership change.

## Milestone D — Expiration, Maintenance, and Administration History

### D1. Enforce expiration semantics

- Treat an unreassigned draft as expired when its recovery deadline is reached, independent of whether physical maintenance has run.
- Refuse listing, viewing, revision, preview, proposal, recovery, and supersession-target operations for expired drafts.
- Ensure a returning member cannot restore an expired draft.
- Record one effective expiration event without exposing expired content or metadata through the web application.

### D2. Establish postal maintenance processing

- Compose a Maintenance institution that owns governed job contracts, dispatch policy, and observable run outcomes without embedding scheduling or transport details in its constitutional identity.
- Define versioned Post Office command contracts for requesting a named maintenance job and correlated outcome contracts for completion or failure.
- Treat postal acceptance as custody of the command rather than proof of collection, dispatch, processing, or success.
- Add a cancellable background facility that collects authorized maintenance commands and dispatches registered bounded workers.
- Persist a run ledger keyed by operation identifier so collection, retry, redelivery, restart, and overlapping dispatch cannot duplicate a completed effect.
- Keep message transport, polling cadence, concurrency limits, and worker hosting replaceable infrastructure concerns.

### D3. Implement expired-draft maintenance

- Register an expired-draft purge worker that identifies and removes a bounded batch of expired content from the Workbench provider.
- Make the worker retryable, idempotent, cancellation-aware, and safe when multiple workers or runs overlap.
- Preserve the minimal administration evidence required to explain expiration without retaining purged draft content.
- Continue safely after individual item failures and complete the run as partial or failed without claiming success for unpurged items.
- Allow a scheduler or authorized operator to trigger the job only by posting its command through the Post Office.
- Post a correlated outcome and safely request follow-up work when another batch remains.

### D4. Present administration history

- Replace the administration-history placeholder with a Maintainer-only, read-only chronological view.
- Include bootstrap, rename, observed membership and role changes, identity-property changes, recovery start/cancellation, reassignment, and expiration.
- Clearly distinguish SSO-observed source events from actions performed by a named Maintainer.
- Preserve effective historical identity values while showing current display names where the stories require them.
- Reveal no private draft content, SSO credentials, raw claims, or control-plane audit details.

### D5. Complete operational and failure guidance

- Show final-Maintainer, invalid configuration, unavailable directory, incomplete synchronization, and maintenance failures on the appropriate administrative surfaces.
- Keep ordinary Members out of settings, recovery, history, and maintenance details while preserving their roster access.
- Verify that application history is never presented as a replacement for the SSO audit log.
- Preserve clear retry paths without application-side authority bypasses or destructive recovery controls.

### QA Checkpoint D — Expiry and Administration

- A draft becomes inaccessible on the exact deadline through every author, proposal, recovery, and direct-access path before purge runs.
- Postal acceptance remains distinguishable from collection, worker dispatch, and successful job completion.
- Maintenance purges expired content once across command redelivery, worker retry, restart, cancellation, concurrency, and partial provider failure.
- Run-ledger and correlated outcome records make completed, partial, and failed maintenance runs observable without exposing expired content.
- Administration history is ordered, durable, correctly attributed, and visible only to active Maintainers.
- Final-Maintainer and directory/configuration failures are prominent and fail closed without blocking allowed ordinary Member reads.
- No administration or discovery surface reveals expired content or restricted recovery metadata.
